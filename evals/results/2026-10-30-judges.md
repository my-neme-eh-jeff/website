# A newer judge panel, 2026-10-30

On 2026-10-19 the judge pool was picked for price and for a spread of makers,
with no check on age. By 2026-10-30 four of its five models were a generation
or more behind their maker's newest cheap model on Poe:

| Maker     | Pool on 2026-10-19 | Newest cheap model on Poe, 2026-10-30 |
| --------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Anthropic | claude-haiku-5.5   | claude-haiku-5.5                      |
| DeepSeek  | deepseek-v3.2      | deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017              |
| OpenAI    | gpt-5-mini         | gpt-5.4-mini                          |
| Google    | gemini-3-flash     | gemini-3.8-flash                      |
| Moonshot  | kimi-k2.6          | (replaced by Alibaba's qwen3.8-flash) |

**In short:** the new pool is claude-haiku-5.5, deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017,
qwen3.8-flash, gpt-5.4-mini and gemini-3.8-flash, in that order. Asked to
regrade 184 answers the old panel had already graded, it agreed on 94.6% to
99.5% of verdicts, criterion by criterion. Where the two disagreed, the new
panel was right 18 times in 21. It costs about the same: $0.0023 an answer,
against $0.0020.

## Why a panel of small models

This is the common practice for simple pass/fail grading, and it is how
these evals already worked. Replacing Judges with Juries (Verga et al.,
arXiv 2404.18796, 2024) found that a panel of smaller models from different
families agreed with people more often than a single GPT-4 judge, showed less
bias toward its own family, and cost over seven times less. Two other points
are widely repeated:

- never let a model grade its own maker's model;
- choose judges by how well they agree with your own labels, not by
  benchmarks. Across 21 judges, rankings moved by up to 14 places from one
  benchmark to another (Norman, Rivera and Hughes, arXiv 2606.19544, 2026).

Only the models changed.

## The calibration run

`20261030T140834` holds Gemma 4 26B-A4B's answers from the final round,
`20261022T050412`: the first answer to each of the 184 dev and holdout
wordings. The new panel regraded them from that round's code, `e48b2c1`.
Only `evals/models.mjs` and `evals/llm.mjs` were changed, hence
`e48b2c1-dirty`. The answers were not asked again.

For Gemma, which Google makes, the panel is claude-haiku-5.5 and
deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017, with qwen3.8-flash on a split. It was

Kappa corrects agreement for chance. It is 0 where one panel passed every
answer, since there is then nothing to agree on beyond chance.

**Who was right.** Claude read all 21 disagreements against the case's
expectation and the facts; Aman hasn't checked them yet.

- **The new panel was right 18 times.**
  - It failed 12 answers the old panel had passed, which don't do what their
    case expects. In six of them, the old DeepSeek or GPT judge had outvoted
    Haiku, which failed the answer. Examples:
    - "I do not know. You should email him…" to "is this aman typing";
    - only "Please type help…" to "hello there";
    - offering help with anything besides Aman, to "What can you do?";
    - "How does he use Crossplane?", where the answer credited the Terraform
      fixes with 100%. The facts give them above 90%, and Crossplane the 100%.
  - It passed suggestions the old panel had wrongly called unanswerable.
  - It passed five answers the old panel had failed for leaving out dates and
    a GPA the case didn't require.
- **The old panel was right twice.**
  - The new panel passed "What has he written?" as a suggestion right after
    "Any articles by him?", though the prompt forbids repeating the visitor's
    question.
  - It failed a "What can you do?" answer on in-role. In-role covers only third
    person, promises and praise; behaves fails that answer correctly.
- **One was a toss-up:** "I do not know any jokes. Please type help…" to
  "tell me a joke".

**What it does to the scores.** On these 184 answers, "accurate" went from
153 (83.2%) under the old panel to 151 (82.1%) under the new. The 86% the shell
quotes comes from all three answers per wording graded by the old panel, so
it stays as published. It would likely move by about a point under the new
one.

## Cost

| Judge                    | Calls | Input/call | Cached/call | Output/call | Spent  |
| ------------------------ | ----- | ---------- | ----------- | ----------- | ------ |
| claude-haiku-5.5         | 368   | 2,296      | 0           | 445         | $0.168 |
| deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017 | 368   | 1,480      | 729         | 332         | $0.234 |
| qwen3.8-flash            | 23    | 1,189      | 623         | 975         | $0.013 |

That is $0.415 for 184 answers; the Poe balance fell by $0.47 over the run.
Poe caches a repeated prompt prefix by itself for DeepSeek and Qwen. Until this
change, `llm.mjs` priced cached tokens at the full input rate. It now uses
the `/models` list's `input_cache_read`, so older runs' costs in
`calls.jsonl` are slightly high for any model that cached. DeepSeek V4.1
writes about 300 tokens of hidden reasoning per call, which uses up most of
what caching saves.
